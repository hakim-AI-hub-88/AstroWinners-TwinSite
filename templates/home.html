Analog terrain to explore

The problem

Every mission rehearses on Earth first. Finding where is slow, manual work.

Rovers, instruments and crews are tested at Earth analog sites, places whose terrain and climate resemble a target on the Moon or Mars. Choosing those sites usually means reading scattered datasets by hand, with little record of why one site beat another.

TwinSite turns that choice into a repeatable, inspectable analysis: pick a target, and it scores real Earth terrain against it, ranks the closest twins and shows every number behind the ranking.

  • Compare

    Feature-by-feature similarity. Slope, roughness, relief, day and night temperature and albedo on a shared normalized scale, with uncertainty-aware weights.

  • Explain

    Nothing hidden. Each score breaks down into the features that agree, the features that differ, missing data and an evidence-coverage confidence.

  • Prepare

    From ranking to field dossier. Refined test points, rover slope checks, validation against published analog sites and a printable mission report.

How it works

One traceable path from a target to a report

Deterministic code computes every number. AI agents only interpret and explain, and must cite evidence that exists.

  1. 01

    Target

    Choose a preset such as Jezero Crater or Apollo 15, or click any Moon or Mars region on the globe.

  2. 02

    Data

    Bundled harmonized NASA grids, plus optional live acquisition through the platform's NASA connectors.

  3. 03

    Cleaning

    Pick a profile (raw, standard, strict or robust) that controls flagged, estimated and outlier cells.

  4. 04

    Analysis

    Weighted similarity across Atacama and Merzouga, rover slope filter, refinement and PCA / K-means views.

  5. 05

    Agents

    13 roles curate sources, review quality, audit the process and write evidence-cited findings.

  6. 06

    Report

    A mission dossier with rankings, maps, validation and methodology, ready to print or save as PDF.

The agent system

13 agents. Every number stays deterministic.

Six roles are deterministic code: they curate sources, gate quality, audit the trace and apply the release gate. Seven roles use free-tier AI providers through a multi-provider router to interpret, review and explain.

AI claims must cite evidence IDs from the run's evidence packet; claims that cite unknown IDs are rejected. AI can never change a score or release a result. When no provider is configured, the AI roles report a fallback instead of pretending.
